Abstract
This project focuses on the design and management of sensor databases
to integrate and optimize AI models in food processing. The system will
collect, store, and organize sensor data efficiently, ensuring its validation for
subsequent visualization and use in predictive models. With an emphasis on
data quality, scalability, and accessibility, the project will ensure the data is
suitable for feeding AI models, improving outcomes in food processing and
other industries.

1.1.1 Introduction
In recent years, the adoption of machine learning (ML) has increased across
industries. The global ML market is projected to reach USD 113.1 billion by
2025, and could expand further to USD 503.4 billion by 2030, growing at a
compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 34.8 %[1]. Meanwhile, an IBM
survey from November 2023 found that 42% of enterprise-scale companies
have actively deployed AI—including ML—in key business functions, and
another 40% are exploring its implementation [2].
The primary goal is to develop a generalised ontology-driven data in-
gestion and management system, motivated by the needs of the Institute
of Research and Technology in Food (IRTA) and Ametller Origen. These

•Data Warehouse : A large storage system used to collect, store, and
analyse data from different sources, often used for business intelligence

1.1.3 Problem Identification
With the foundational terms defined, we now turn to identifying the main
problem that motivates this work, particularly within the context of data
usage in the agri-food industry.
Agri-food companies must improve efficiency while ensuring quality,
traceability, and food safety. To achieve these goals, it is essential to collect
(sense) and standardise data in a way that guarantees consistent, reliable,
and interpretable information across all stages of the production process.
High-quality, robust products depend heavily on accurate and timely data.
However, current industry practices often rely on disparate sensor systems
and manual data entry, which introduce variability, errors, and inefficiencies.
Without a unified and standardised approach to data collection, companies
face challenges in ensuring product consistency, responding to anomalies,
and making data-driven decisions. Although real-time sensor data has
the potential to improve process optimisation, the core challenge lies in
structuring and managing raw data for AI applications. Many current
solutions focus on isolated sensor networks but lack a unified framework for
organising, ingesting, and integrating heterogeneous data sources [4].
Thus, ontology-based data structuring is crucial. Not only facilitates
interoperability between systems, it also ensures that all captured data
adhere to common semantic standards. This improves data quality, simplifies
downstream processing, and supports AI-driven analysis, such as predictive
modelling of food properties or early detection of quality issues.
Currently, much of the collected data is stored in unstructured formats
such as CSV or JSON, making retrieval, aggregation, and analysis complex
and error-prone. The project proposes a general and reusable ontology
model that enables efficient data organisation and ingestion, making it more
accessible and usable for advanced analytics.
Although IoT and real-time data capture may support this effort, they
are not the core focus of the project. Instead, the emphasis is on building a
standardised and scalable data framework that can later incorporate such
elements if needed.

1.2.1 State of Art
To better understand the context in which this project operates, it is impor-
tant to examine the current state of the art in data management for wireless
sensor networks and AI applications.
In the context of wireless sensor networks, several key technologies and
approaches stand out to improve data management efficiency. Energy effi-
ciency and local processing are fundamental, since distributed databases and
local aggregation techniques help optimise the use of limited resources [5]. In
situations where complex relationships are handled, such as in multimedia
sensor networks, graph databases are ideal to represent and process these
scenarios [6]. Furthermore, time series databases are essential in Internet
of Things (IoT) applications, as they efficiently manage time-stamped data,
which is crucial for analysis [7][8]. Finally, migrating to NoSQL databases
offers a flexible and scalable solution that enables the effective management
of heterogeneous sensor data [9].
1.2.2 Solution
Building on the limitations identified in current approaches, the proposed so-
lution aims to address these challenges through an ontology-based framework
tailored to the agri-food industry.
The existing literature explores various database technologies, but most
lack a holistic approach to integrate AI models with structured data ingestion.
In addition, few studies focus on the agri-food sector, where structured data
are crucial to solving real-world issues such as ensuring product quality,
improving traceability, and enabling predictive analysis for crop yields or
food safety. In this context, the agri-food sector faces significant obstacles
such as inconsistent data collection methods, limited scalability of existing
systems, and challenges in integrating disparate data sources from various
stages of the food supply chain [4].
This project seeks to address these specific challenges by proposing a
generalised ontology-driven approach to structuring sensor data, developing
an automated ingestion pipeline. The project ensures data integrity and
accessibility while minimising the risk of human error in data entry and
processing. In addition, the system includes a scalable data storage solution

making for stakeholders within the agri-food industry.

ology that can be applied in various domains, ensuring both flexibility and

1.3.1 Objectives and Sub-objectives
The primary and supporting goals of the project are described in the following,
each contributing to the development of a robust and effective sensor data
management system.
The primary objective is the design, implementation and management of
a sensor database based on ontology that enables structured data ingestion
and AI-driven analysis. To achieve this, the following sub-objectives must
be met:
•Ontology Development: The main objective is to create a standard-
ised and comprehensive framework that structures raw sensor data,
ensuring consistency and compatibility between various datasets. The
expected result is a well-defined ontology that enables efficient and
effective data organisation, supporting the integration of various sensor
inputs while enhancing data accuracy and traceability.
•Database Specification and Design: The goal is to develop a
scalable, flexible, and efficient database solution capable of storing and
querying large volumes of sensor data. The expected outcome is a

with a focus on scalability to accommodate future data growth and
adaptability to new data types or sources.
•Automated Data Ingestion Process: The objective is to implement
a system for periodic and automatic data ingestion that incorporates
ontological validations to ensure the quality and consistency of the
data incoming. The expected result is a fully automated ingestion
pipeline that seamlessly collects and processes data while maintaining
high standards of data integrity and minimising the need for manual

•Data Warehouse Management: The goal is to design a system to

control, backup strategies, and data distribution mechanisms to en-

secure and well-managed database infrastructure that guarantees high
availability, data security, and smooth operational performance.

1.4 Methodology and Rigour
To ensure that the project is developed in a structured and iterative manner,
an Agile-based methodology will be followed. This section describes the
development framework, tools, and practices chosen that support consis-
tent progress, quality assurance, and collaboration throughout the project
lifecycle.
The project will follow an Agile methodology based on Kanban, aligning
with industry best practices and methodologies studied to a degree. Kanban
optimises workflow by breaking tasks into phases and setting limits on work
in progress, making it particularly efficient for a single-developer environment.
The workflow will consist of the following phases:
•TO DO : Tasks that need to be completed.
•DOING : Active development tasks.
•TESTING : Features under validation and bug fixing.
•TO REVIEW : Tasks awaiting feedback or approval.
•DONE : Completed and accepted tasks.
In addition, the methodology will incorporate sprints for structure devel-
opment. Sprint Planning will be conducted at the beginning of each sprint

of the sprint, both Sprint Review and Retrospective meetings will be held
to assess progress and refine the work methodology, enhancing efficiency in

the collaborators will validate them prior to deployment. In our case, daily
scrum is not going to be held, since there is one single developer [Figure

Hierarchical Representation of Agri-Food Products
A key aspect of the ontology’s design is the hierarchical classification of
samples, which enables scalable and semantically rich representation of food
products.
One of the main objectives has been to properly organise the samples that
are being analysed, which may be recipes or raw materials. Both are modelled
as classes (Recipe and RawMaterial) and are structured hierarchically through
subclasses (Gazpacho, Tomato, Vegetable, DairyCompound, etc.). This
approach allows for the generalisation of shared properties, such as those
common to mild gazpacho and pumpkin cream, without sacrificing specificity
when needed.
A clear decision was made in favour of a class-based ontology, avoiding
the use of individuals except when absolutely necessary. This decision not
only facilitates understanding and visual representation (e.g., in hierarchical
diagrams), but also allows for better validation of structures during data
ingestion and more agile model maintenance.
The class Vegetable , for example, groups subsets such as Tomato ,Garlic ,
orRedPepper , while other classes such as DairyCompound can represent
recipe ingredients that do not share a plant-based origin.

During development, the suitability of maintaining intermediate depth

•Structured and hierarchical model : where each parameter is a

ontological control.

To ensure fault tolerance and continuous availability in production environ-

•Secondary Nodes (2 replicas) : Replicate data from the primary

•Router Server (mongos + balancer) : In a shard environment,

•Shards : Each shard is itself a Replica Set, ensuring redundancy.
•Sharding Key : Carefully selected to ensure an even distribution and

data warehouse).

as follows:
•Compatibility with both relational and non-relational databases.
•Ease of use for non-technical users.
•Support for SQL and visual queries.
•Ability to create interactive dashboards.
•Technical requirements for deployment and maintenance.
•Developer community and available documentation.

Apache Superset is a very robust tool, developed in Python and React,

two main limitations for this project. First, it is deeply integrated with
SQL models, which limits or prevents its direct use with NoSQL databases

launched with minimal resources.
Furthermore, Metabase’s open source model ensures technological inde-
pendence and future adaptability, free from licencing restrictions. Its low
learning curve, combined with the ability to connect simultaneously to both
databases used, makes it a highly versatile option for agile, multidisciplinary
environments.
After a detailed comparative analysis, Metabase was selected as the
main visualisation tool for the project. This decision is based on several
factors:
•Its direct compatibility with the system’s databases (MongoDB and
PostgreSQL).
•Its user-friendly interface is suitable for non-technical users.
•Its lightweight and easy-to-maintain architecture.
•Most importantly, its open-source nature, which allows integration,
extension, and customisation without relying on closed or expensive
solutions.
While Apache Superset remains a powerful alternative, its exclusive focus
on the relational model and its higher operational complexity make it less
suitable for the project’s current goals and resources.

functionalities are currently in a stub state. A future goal is to design
a fully modular and configurable data cleaning pipeline, allowing users
to define transformation rules and preprocessing logic specific to each
project.
7.Big Data Integration with PySpark: For scenarios involving
large-scale data, future versions may integrate PySpark for distributed
querying and parallel data processing, especially when datasets ex-
ceed system memory. Although Pandas remains suitable for small
to medium datasets, PySpark would enable scalability and efficient
data handling, including direct integration with MongoDB and other
distributed storage systems.
These future enhancements aim to improve the overall robustness, scala-
bility, and adaptability of the platform, allowing it to meet the demands of
increasingly complex and data-intensive projects.
